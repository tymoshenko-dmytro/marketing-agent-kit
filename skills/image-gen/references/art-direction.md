# Art direction for generated marketing images

Rules collected from real review rounds on landing pages, blog articles, banners and video. Each one is a rejection that happened at least once.

## Content

1. **Illustrate the content literally.** The image shows the thing the section is about. Greeked placeholders — a desk with blurred papers, abstract "documents", stock-photo smiles — read as generic filler and get rejected. If the section lists five bonus materials, show five documents with their real short titles.
2. **One image, one idea.** If the caption needs two sentences to explain the image, the image is doing too much.
3. **Show the product itself** when you can. A screenshot or a faithful UI mockup beats a metaphor about the product. A visual metaphor is fine only when it is literally true to the words next to it.
4. **Text in the image doesn't repeat the copy.** If the visual already shows the certifications, the languages or the event types, remove them from the headline — the copy adds only what is new.

## Text inside images

5. **At most two pieces of text**, each a few words.
6. **Check every letter.** Zoom in on every rendered word before showing the image. Re-render or edit if a single character is wrong.
7. **Numbers in images must be the real numbers** from the copy or the source. Never let the model invent figures, prices, ratings or logos.

## Screenshots and crops

8. **A crop shows the block its caption talks about**, not the page header above it.
9. **A screenshot of your own page goes with a link to that page** in the caption. Screenshots of other sites don't get a link.
10. **No two images in a row** without text between them, and no full-page screenshots — both read as broken layout.
11. **Every article needs a cover image** (the featured image for the CMS).
