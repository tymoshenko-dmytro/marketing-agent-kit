# Before / after

Each pair is a pattern that got rejected in a real review, with the kind of rewrite that shipped and why. The products are fictional; the patterns are real. Add your own at the end.

## Hooks

| Before | After | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Nobody types anything. | An AI note-taker for your sales calls. | A mechanism as the opening line lands on nothing: the reader doesn't know what the product is yet. |
| Under a minute from question to report. | Ask your analytics a question in plain English — get the report in under a minute. | Who, what, why? Name the thing first, then the number. |
| Half your churn comes from customers you never onboarded. | Onboard every new customer automatically, in their first hour. | A pain the reader has never felt about themselves is not a pain. |
| The candidate books the interview at 3 a.m. | Candidate in Tokyo, recruiter in Berlin? The scheduler finds a slot that works for both. | The situation has to be logical and state both the ask and the obstacle. |
| Your Spanish trial conversion is half your US one. | Your trials abroad convert worse than at home. | A specific country narrows the audience for no reason — unless the slot is deliberately swapped per card. |

## Banners

| Before | After | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Agencies charge $5k a month… / Get the same for 80% less | Save $4,000 a month on reporting. | One direct thought, one number. |
| Replace 1,200 spreadsheets. | (removed the number) | "Why 1,200?" — a number the reader can't place is noise. |
| Save 6 hours a week. | Save 6 hours a week per recruiter. | Name the object of the saving. |
| One setup for your whole hiring calendar. | One tool schedules every interview. Set it up once. | The subject is the product, the verb is what it does. |
| Dashboards, replaced by one Slack message. (as the headline) | Headline: Know your numbers without opening a dashboard. Sub: One Slack message every morning. | The headline is the reader's problem or benefit; the mechanism goes in the sub. |
| SOC 2, GDPR, SSO — built for enterprise teams. (next to certification badges) | Built for enterprise teams. | The visual already shows the badges; text adds only what is new. |

## Email

| Before | After | Why |
|---|---|---|
| …which channels actually work, what turned out to be a waste, and what we're each seeing in the market. | …to compare notes on growth channels. | A rhetorical triple is the first thing readers recognise as AI. |
| Not a sales call. | (removed) | A dramatic fragment reads as a template. |
| people doing my job in companies like ours | people responsible for growth | Literal beats clever. |

## Landing pages

| Before | After | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Captions on every video (WCAG-relevant for schools and enterprises) | Captions on every video | The standard wasn't in the source and adds nothing for the reader. |
| Other tools break on long files, mixed formats, messy data… | Handles long files, mixed formats and messy data — the cases where accuracy matters most. | Don't describe the category negatively; show the product on hard cases. |
| The same speed for every customer, guaranteed. | Kept as fast as possible. | Don't guarantee what you can't. |
| A block that converted, rewritten "to fit the new page" with new claims about integrations | The original block, word for word; only the headline and screenshot changed | Proven copy stays; rewriting it added unverifiable claims. |

## Support

| Before | After | Why |
|---|---|---|
| You'll keep access for another week, no need to use it — it's there if you change your mind. | You keep access until your subscription ends. If I help you set it up today, would you give it a try? | No guessed timeframes; retention means using the product, not parking it. |
| Most people who cancel hit one snag in setup — let me make sure you're not one of them! | Want me to walk you through the setup? | Pushy and performative; make the offer and ask. |
